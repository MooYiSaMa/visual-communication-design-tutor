## 中文说明

### 项目简介

**Visual Communication Design Tutor** 是一个专门服务于视觉传达设计学习、毕业设计、独立项目和作品集准备的研究型导师 Skill。

它将项目诊断、主题探索、设计研究、概念发展、视觉转译、形式实验、视觉系统、专业批评和作品集叙事组织为一条连续的工作链，帮助设计者从模糊感受或初步观察出发，逐步建立有证据、有逻辑、有作者性的视觉项目。

它不以“快速生成一个看起来像设计的结果”为目标，也不会在研究不足时直接替用户决定风格、配色或最终方案。它更关注以下问题：

- 你究竟在研究什么，而不只是讨论什么主题？
- 你的判断由什么证据支持？
- 研究如何真正改变了视觉形式？
- 你为什么选择这个方向，又为什么放弃其他方向？
- 最终成果是否能够形成可延展的视觉系统？
- 项目中是否能看见你的经验、感受和设计判断？

### 适用对象

这个 Skill 适合：

- 还没有明确选题的视觉传达设计学生；
- 正在进行毕业设计或独立项目的设计者；
- 想把个人感受、文学经验或生活观察转化为视觉研究的人；
- 项目已经开始，但不知道下一步该做什么的人；
- 需要严格检查概念、形式和制作质量的人；
- 正在整理申请作品集或项目案例的人。

你不需要一开始就拥有成熟的概念。你可以从一句感受、一段文字、一个观察、一组图像、一种材料，或一个尚未解决的问题开始。

### 核心方法

#### 1. 研究先于形式

视觉形式不应该只由关键词、流行风格或个人偏好决定。Skill 会先确认项目的具体情境、已有证据、未知问题和作者立场，再判断形式如何从这些内容中被推导出来。

#### 2. 视觉转译，而不是文字配图

研究内容需要经过选择、压缩、重组和形式化，才能成为视觉语言。Skill 会检查研究如何改变字体、图像、构图、节奏、材料、媒介和观看方式，而不是把研究文字简单配上一组图片。

#### 3. 过程证据与最终成果同样重要

成熟的设计项目不仅展示最终效果，还要说明：观察到了什么、形成了什么判断、测试过哪些可能性、为什么放弃某些方向，以及最终形式如何回应问题。

#### 4. 保留作者判断

Skill 可以帮助用户比较方向、发现漏洞和建立评价标准，但不会替用户完成全部设计判断。用户的经验、感受、选择、限制和价值立场，应当在项目中保持可见。

### 能力范围

| 能力模块 | 具体作用 |
|---|---|
| 项目诊断 | 判断项目当前处于哪个阶段，找出最先需要解决的瓶颈。 |
| 主题探索 | 将个人感受、生活观察和兴趣转化为可研究、可设计的问题。 |
| 设计研究 | 组织观察、访谈、文本、图像、档案、案例和材料实验，形成可使用的证据。 |
| 概念发展 | 从证据中识别模式、矛盾和张力，形成可以被论证的概念与立场。 |
| 视觉转译 | 将抽象概念转化为字体、图像、构图、色彩、材料、动态和媒介规则。 |
| 形式实验 | 围绕明确假设进行实验，并记录比较、失败和学习。 |
| 视觉系统 | 将单张图像或单个页面发展为可延展的视觉语法和一致性规则。 |
| 专业批评 | 从概念、证据、视觉逻辑、系统性、制作质量和作者性进行评审。 |
| 作品集叙事 | 将研究、判断、实验、发展和最终成果组织为清晰的案例叙事。 |

### 九阶段工作流程

```text
诊断 → 探索 → 研究 → 概念 → 转译 → 实验 → 发展 → 批评 → 作品集
```

#### 01. 诊断

确认项目的具体情境、相关对象、未知问题、已有证据、媒介条件、限制和当前阶段。先判断真正的瓶颈，再决定下一步行动。

#### 02. 探索

从个人感受和初步兴趣出发，比较多个潜在方向，区分“主题”“情绪表达”“研究问题”和“视觉命题”。

#### 03. 研究

建立研究地图，收集一手观察与可靠参考，标记来源、语境和局限，并从资料中寻找关系，而不是简单罗列事实。

#### 04. 概念

把研究中的模式或矛盾转化为洞察、立场和概念命题，同时检查概念是否只是重复关键词或熟悉的视觉套路。

#### 05. 转译

回答“这个概念为什么需要这样的视觉形式”，将立场落实为视觉语言、媒介逻辑和观看机制。

#### 06. 实验

围绕具体假设进行字体、图像、材料、构图、动态、尺度、顺序和媒介实验，并保留可比较的样本。

#### 07. 发展

从有效实验中提炼规则、状态、变体和制作方法，建立能够持续生成新内容的视觉系统。

#### 08. 批评

根据项目意图和评价标准检查作品，区分概念问题、视觉问题、制作问题和叙事问题，提出下一轮可执行的修改。

#### 09. 作品集

将项目组织为有节奏的案例：背景与问题、研究证据、关键判断、实验过程、视觉系统、最终成果和反思。

### 如何使用

#### 基本调用

```text
$visual-communication-design-tutor
```

也可以直接描述你的任务：

```text
我想做一个关于“感受力”的视觉传达毕业设计。
我现在只有个人感受和一些文字，请先帮我判断项目处于哪个阶段，
不要直接给我海报风格或配色方案。
```

#### 还没有选题

提供个人经历、持续关注的现象、强烈感受和已有材料，让 Skill 帮你判断它们能否发展为研究问题，以及下一步需要补充什么证据。

#### 项目卡住

提供当前已有内容、做过的尝试和不满意的地方。Skill 会定位最早尚未解决的瓶颈，而不是继续无目的地产生新想法。

#### 需要专业批评

提供作品图、文字说明、实验记录或页面结构，并指定希望检查的维度：概念、证据、形式逻辑、系统性、制作质量、作者性或叙事。

#### 准备作品集

提供项目过程材料和最终成果，让 Skill 帮你重建证据链、筛选关键节点、安排阅读节奏，并识别过程叙事中的空缺。

### 推荐输入格式

```text
项目主题或个人感受：

我观察到的具体情境：

我已有的材料或证据：

我已经尝试过什么：

我觉得哪里不满意：

时间、媒介、技术和格式限制：

我希望下一步得到什么：
```

输入越具体，反馈越能落到可执行的设计动作。如果信息不足，Skill 会优先提出真正会改变下一步判断的问题。

### 输出特点

一次高质量的指导通常会包含：

1. 当前模式与项目阶段；
2. 一句话诊断和主要瓶颈；
3. 支撑判断的证据；
4. 需要验证的关键假设；
5. 少量但必要的问题；
6. 一个有边界的下一步任务；
7. 明确的完成标准；
8. 对项目状态或作品集叙事的更新建议。

它会尽量把“你可以再想想”“这个方向很有潜力”转化为可观察、可比较、可验证的设计动作。

### 使用边界

- 不把关键词直接等同于视觉形式；
- 不在研究不足时假装已经形成洞察；
- 不用大量风格参考代替个人判断；
- 不把随机变化称为实验；
- 不只评价“好不好看”，而要说明它是否回应问题；
- 不制造虚假的观察、访谈、来源或项目经历；
- 不抹平项目中的矛盾、限制和未解决问题；
- 不代替用户做最终设计判断。

## English

### Overview

**Visual Communication Design Tutor** is a research-led mentor Skill for visual communication design students, graduation-project authors, independent designers, and portfolio applicants.

It connects project diagnosis, topic exploration, design research, concept development, visual translation, formal experimentation, visual-system building, critique, and portfolio storytelling into one continuous workflow. It helps designers move from a vague feeling or initial observation toward a visual project with evidence, logic, and authorship.

The Skill is not designed to quickly generate something that merely looks like design. It does not decide a style, color palette, or final solution before the project has enough evidence. Instead, it asks:

- What are you actually investigating, beyond the topic?
- What evidence supports your judgment?
- How has research changed the visual form?
- Why did you choose this direction and reject others?
- Can the final outcome operate as an expandable visual system?
- Can the designer’s experience, position, and judgment remain visible?

### Who It Is For

This Skill is useful for:

- visual communication design students without a clear topic;
- designers developing a graduation or independent project;
- people translating personal feelings, literary experience, or everyday observations into visual research;
- designers who have started a project but do not know what to do next;
- students seeking rigorous critique of concept, form, and craft;
- applicants organizing a design-school portfolio or project case study.

You do not need a mature concept at the beginning. You can start with one feeling, a piece of writing, an observation, a group of images, a material, or an unresolved question.

### Core Principles

#### 1. Research before form

Visual form should not be determined only by keywords, popular styles, or personal preference. The Skill first examines the situation, evidence, unknown question, and authorial position before deriving a formal direction.

#### 2. Visual translation instead of decoration

Research must be selected, condensed, reorganized, and formalized before becoming visual language. The Skill checks how research changes typography, image, composition, rhythm, material, medium, and viewing conditions instead of simply decorating written research with images.

#### 3. Process evidence matters as much as outcome

A mature project shows more than the final result. It explains what was observed, how judgments were formed, which alternatives were tested, why certain directions were rejected, and how the final form responds to the question.

#### 4. Preserve authorship

The Skill can compare directions, identify weaknesses, and establish criteria, but it does not replace the designer’s judgment. Experience, feeling, choice, constraint, and values should remain visible in the project.

### Capability Map

| Capability | What it does |
|---|---|
| Project diagnosis | Identifies the current project stage and the earliest unresolved bottleneck. |
| Topic exploration | Turns personal feelings, observations, and interests into researchable design questions. |
| Design research | Organizes observations, conversations, texts, images, archives, references, and material tests into evidence. |
| Concept development | Finds patterns, contradictions, and tensions in evidence, then forms defensible concepts and positions. |
| Visual translation | Translates abstract ideas into rules for typography, image, composition, color, material, motion, and medium. |
| Formal experimentation | Runs hypothesis-driven experiments and records comparison, failure, and learning. |
| Visual systems | Develops a visual grammar and consistency rules beyond a single image or page. |
| Professional critique | Reviews concept, evidence, visual logic, system, craft, and authorship. |
| Portfolio storytelling | Organizes research, decisions, experiments, development, and outcomes into a clear case study. |

### Nine-Stage Workflow

```text
Diagnosis → Exploration → Research → Concept → Translation
→ Experimentation → Development → Critique → Portfolio
```

#### 01. Diagnosis

Clarify the situation, people, unknown question, evidence, medium, constraints, and current stage. Identify the real bottleneck before choosing the next action.

#### 02. Exploration

Compare potential directions from personal feelings and initial interests. Distinguish a topic, emotional expression, research question, and visual proposition.

#### 03. Research

Build a research map, collect primary observations and credible references, record context and limits, and look for relationships rather than listing facts.

#### 04. Concept

Turn patterns or contradictions into insights, positions, and concept propositions. Check that the concept is not merely repeating a keyword or familiar visual cliché.

#### 05. Translation

Answer why the concept needs a particular visual form, then translate the position into visual language, medium logic, and a mode of viewing.

#### 06. Experimentation

Run focused experiments in typography, image, material, composition, motion, scale, sequence, and medium while preserving comparable samples.

#### 07. Development

Extract rules, states, variations, and production methods from successful experiments to build a visual system that can generate new content.

#### 08. Critique

Evaluate the work against its intent and criteria. Distinguish conceptual, visual, production, and narrative problems before proposing the next revision.

#### 09. Portfolio

Condense the project into a paced case study: context and question, research evidence, key decisions, experiments, visual system, final outcomes, and reflection.

### How to Use

#### Basic invocation

```text
$visual-communication-design-tutor
```

You can also describe the task directly:

```text
I want to develop a visual communication graduation project about sensitivity.
At the moment I only have personal feelings and some writing. Diagnose the current
project stage first. Do not jump directly to poster styles or color palettes.
```

#### No topic yet

Share personal experiences, recurring observations, strong feelings, and existing material. The Skill helps determine whether they can become a research question and what evidence is still missing.

#### The project is stuck

Share what exists, what has been tested, and what feels unsuccessful. The Skill identifies the earliest unresolved bottleneck instead of producing more unfocused ideas.

#### Need a critical review

Provide images, text, experiment logs, or page structures, and specify the dimensions to review: concept, evidence, formal logic, system, craft, authorship, or narrative.

#### Preparing a portfolio

Provide process material and final outcomes. The Skill helps rebuild the evidence chain, select key moments, organize reading rhythm, and identify gaps in the process narrative.

### Recommended Input Format

```text
Project topic or personal feeling:

Specific situation I observed:

Existing material or evidence:

What I have already tried:

What feels unsuccessful:

Time, medium, technical, and format constraints:

What I want to achieve next:
```

The more specific the input, the more actionable the feedback. If information is insufficient, the Skill asks only the questions that can change the next decision.

### Typical Output

A high-quality response will usually include:

1. Current mode and project stage;
2. One-sentence diagnosis and primary bottleneck;
3. Evidence supporting the judgment;
4. Key assumption to test;
5. A small number of necessary questions;
6. One bounded next action;
7. Clear success criteria;
8. Suggested updates to project state or portfolio narrative.

The Skill aims to turn vague encouragement such as “think more” or “this direction has potential” into observable, comparable, and testable design actions.

### Boundaries

- Do not equate a keyword directly with a visual form.
- Do not pretend that an insight exists when evidence is insufficient.
- Do not use stylistic references as a substitute for judgment.
- Do not call random variation experimentation.
- Do not judge only whether something looks good; explain whether it responds to the question.
- Do not invent observations, conversations, sources, or project experiences.
- Do not erase contradictions, constraints, or unresolved questions.
- Do not replace the user’s final design judgment.
