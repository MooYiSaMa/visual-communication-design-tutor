# 08 — Visual Portfolio Case Study

## Narrative sequence

1. Context — why this visual/cultural situation matters.
2. Question — what was unknown or contested.
3. Research — what was studied and how.
4. Evidence — selected traces, artifacts, images, text, or archive.
5. Insight — what changed the understanding.
6. Position — what the project argues, asks, or refuses.
7. Visual translation — how the position became formal principles.
8. Experiment — alternatives, hypotheses, and failure.
9. Development — why the direction changed.
10. Visual system — type, image, composition, material, sequence, medium.
11. Outcome — what the audience sees, reads, handles, or watches.
12. Reflection — limits, ethics, production learning, future question.

## Pacing rules

- Open with the tension and position, not a decorative cover.
- Alternate evidence density with visual pauses.
- Make the turning point unmistakable.
- Caption every image with its role in the reasoning.
- Show enough failure to prove selection.
- Keep final images subordinate to the project argument.
- Show production proof when material or craft is central.

## Gate

A reviewer should understand what mattered, what was learned, how evidence became visual language, what changed through experiments, and what the student would investigate next.
