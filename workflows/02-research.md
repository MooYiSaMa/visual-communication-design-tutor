# 02 — Research

## Purpose

Build evidence that can alter the project. Research is not a list of links or references.

## Method selection

| Unknown | Suitable method | Output |
|---|---|---|
| lived behavior | contextual interview / observation | quotes, actions, artifacts |
| change over time | diary / longitudinal check-in | sequence, triggers, coping |
| visual/cultural context | relationship and archive mapping | influences, absences, power |
| existing practice | desk research / case comparison | patterns, gaps, precedents |
| meaning of objects/images | artifact/image elicitation | language, associations, use |
| response to a form | probe or prototype test | behavior, confusion, preference |

Use at least one primary method when the question concerns lived experience. Use secondary/visual references to widen context and formal possibilities, not to replace contact with the situation.

## Research plan

Specify:

- research question and sub-questions;
- participants or materials, with a reasoned sample;
- recruitment/access and consent;
- method, duration, setting, and recording plan;
- risks, privacy, withdrawal, and data storage;
- what evidence would confirm, complicate, or disprove the assumption;
- synthesis date and decision that the research must inform.

## Interview guide

Start with behavior, not opinions:

1. Tell me about the last time ___ happened.
2. What happened immediately before and after?
3. What did you do, and what alternatives did you consider?
4. What made that easy, difficult, visible, or invisible?
5. Which object, image, text, convention, or platform affected the decision?
6. What visual representation would feel inaccurate, extractive, or reductive here?
7. Can you show or describe the artifact/trace involved?

Avoid leading questions, speculative “would you use,” and requests for participants to design the solution.

## Evidence ladder

Keep a log with one row per observation:

| ID | Evidence | Source/context | Interpretation | Confidence | Design implication |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E01 | exact behavior/quote/trace | who/where/when | tentative meaning | low/med/high | what may change |

Never upgrade an interpretation to a fact. Mark the student's inference and seek triangulation.

## Synthesis

1. Transcribe or summarize without premature themes.
2. Code actions, emotions, constraints, workarounds, contradictions, and absences.
3. Cluster by behavior or tension, not by interview question.
4. Compare across people, contexts, and sources.
5. Write candidate insights in the form: “Although ___, people ___ because ___; therefore a design should ___.”
6. Test each insight against the evidence and a disconfirming case.

## Reference taxonomy

Classify every external reference as method, context, precedent, visual language, material process, or audience/sector evidence. Record:

```text
reference → why relevant → transferable principle → boundary/not to copy → design decision
```

RCA archive material can inform critical/contextual positioning; Behance can reveal contemporary project structures and presentation conventions; Pinterest can support visual/method discovery but is weak evidence of lived behavior; Creative Review can provide industry discourse and cases; Core77 can support visual/product precedents. Verify dates, authorship, and claims when they matter.

## Research gate

Move to concept only when there is: a clear question, traceable evidence, at least one pattern or contradiction, a stated uncertainty, and an implication that could change visual form, sequence, medium, or audience reading.
