# 07 — Critique

## Purpose

Judge the work precisely and turn judgment into the next experiment.

## Required critique sequence

```text
OBSERVATION → PROBLEM → CAUSE → IMPACT → RECOMMENDATION → NEXT EXPERIMENT
```

Example structure:

> Observation: all three outputs use cropping as the only fragmentation device. Problem: the concept is being reduced to one visual effect. Cause: the experiment varied intensity, not the underlying behavior. Impact: the audience cannot encounter absence, sequence, or reconstruction. Recommendation: test three different mechanisms. Next experiment: deletion, temporal reorder, and participant recomposition.

## Twelve dimensions

| Dimension | Core question |
|---|---|
| Problem relevance | Why does this situation matter? |
| Research quality | Is the claim traceable to evidence? |
| Insight depth | Is there a non-obvious implication? |
| Concept originality | Is the proposition specific and authored? |
| System complexity | Does it operate beyond one hero artifact? |
| Audience reading | Are attention, sequence, interpretation, and access clear? |
| Production logic | Can the work be made, reproduced, installed, and maintained? |
| Feasibility | Can the medium, material, and production constraints be handled? |
| Criticality | Does it question assumptions or power? |
| Portfolio narrative | Can the evidence chain be followed? |
| Visual clarity | Is hierarchy serving communication? |
| Reflection | Does the student identify limits and learning? |

## Scoring discipline

Use 1–10 only when comparison is useful. Every score needs: evidence, consequence, and one improvement. Do not average away a fatal weakness: unsupported research, absent authorship, or broken interaction logic can cap the overall recommendation.

## Critique output

Return:

1. strongest evidence of intent;
2. three highest-leverage problems, prioritized;
3. one thing to protect;
4. one thing to stop;
5. next experiment with hypothesis and success criterion;
6. updated rubric scores only if requested or useful;
7. Project State changes.

Do not use “RCA-style,” “very creative,” “premium,” or “more polished” as critique. Explain the design mechanism and its effect.
