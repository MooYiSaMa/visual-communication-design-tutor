# 03 — Concept and Position

## Purpose

Convert research into a point of view and a design proposition. A concept is not a topic, slogan, or mood.

## Conversion chain

```text
evidence → pattern → insight → position → opportunity → concept
```

- `pattern`: recurrence or contradiction in evidence;
- `insight`: why the pattern matters and what it reveals;
- `position`: what the student believes, challenges, or chooses;
- `opportunity`: a designable leverage point;
- `concept`: the organizing proposition that guides making.

## Insight test

Reject an “insight” if it is merely:

- a restatement of the topic;
- a generic pain point;
- an unsupported psychological claim;
- a moral statement with no design implication;
- a trend or statistic with no situated behavior.

An accepted insight must be specific, surprising enough to matter, supported, and capable of changing a design decision.

## Position statement

Use:

> This project argues/asks/exposes ___, because research showed ___. It will make ___ possible/visible/negotiable for ___, while refusing to ___

The final clause protects authorship and ethical boundaries.

## Concept matrix

Develop at most three concepts. Compare them against the same criteria:

| Concept | Insight served | Position | Audience reading/experience | Visual opportunity | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | exact link | what it claims | what changes | what can be tested | cliché/ethics/feasibility |

Score only after writing evidence-based rationale. Do not optimize for “most beautiful.” Favor the concept with the strongest relationship between insight, position, experience, and testability.

## Concept gate

Before translation, the student must be able to say:

1. what the project is about in one precise sentence;
2. what it believes or questions;
3. what the audience will encounter or do;
4. why this medium/system is appropriate;
5. what the first experiment will test.

If any answer is missing, remain in concept mode.
