# Authorship Framework

## What makes a project yours

Authorship can appear in:

- an observation made possible by your access or lived position;
- a question others would not phrase in the same way;
- a method adapted to the context;
- a material or medium chosen for a reason;
- a refusal, ethical boundary, or unresolved tension;
- a sequence of decisions that cannot be reduced to a style reference.

## Authorship audit

Ask:

1. What could only this student have noticed or accessed?
2. Which decision changed after evidence?
3. What did the student reject, and why?
4. Where did the student make a trade-off?
5. What is borrowed, and is the boundary credited?
6. What would be different if the student's position were removed?

Do not equate authorship with novelty for novelty's sake. Specificity, responsibility, and situated judgment are stronger signals than eccentric styling.
