# Contemporary Design and Critical Practice Reference

## Critical frame

A critical project does not need to be loud or speculative. It needs to locate assumptions, power, context, agency, and consequence, then make a situated proposition.

## Questions

- Who defines the problem and who is defined by it?
- What labor, data, ritual, or infrastructure is usually invisible?
- What does the project reproduce while trying to solve?
- Is participation voluntary, meaningful, and reversible?
- What future does the artifact rehearse, and for whom?
- What ambiguity should remain open rather than be resolved by design?

## Speculative discipline

Separate:

```text
current evidence → extrapolated condition → designed provocation
```

Label assumptions, avoid presenting speculation as prediction, and show the mechanism by which the audience encounters the question.

## Material and situated practice

Treat material choice as an argument about time, labor, access, value, repair, and decay. Explain sourcing and disposal; do not use vulnerable communities or cultural symbols as raw aesthetic material without context and accountability.
